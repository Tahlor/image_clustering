Business problem

What this team actually does

Before search, browse, reviews, variants, recommendations, or shopping agents can work well, Amazon has to know: What product is this, and how is it related to other products?

Exact identity

Five sellers may describe the same Logitech mouse differently. The catalog should know they refer to the same underlying product.

Variation family

Size 10 and size 11 are not the same sellable item, but may belong under the same base product.

Dangerous lookalikes

128 GB vs 256 GB, current vs previous model, 1-pack vs 12-pack, phone vs case. Similarity is not identity.

Architecture

The system to keep in your head

seller/product update ↓ normalize title / brand / attributes / images ↓ multimodal product embedding ↓ ANN retrieval: top plausible existing candidates + deterministic candidates from IDs/rules ↓ multimodal cross-encoder / VLM reranker ↓ P(exact same), P(variant), P(other relation), P(different) ↓ graph/group consistency ↓ calibration + business policy ↓ automate / gather evidence / human review / abstain ↓ safe catalog update + monitoring + training feedback

Why this decomposition?

  • Retrieval makes billion-scale search tractable.
  • Reranking spends expensive reasoning only on plausible candidates.
  • Graph consistency catches contradictory pairwise decisions.
  • Calibration/policy turns a model score into a safe business action.
  • Feedback makes future training data better.
“I'd start with high-recall candidate generation, then a richer pair/group model, then calibrated selective automation. I'd evaluate retrieval and final decisions separately and explicitly route the ambiguous tail rather than forcing every case.”
Supervision

Where training data comes from

First define the relationship label

EXACT_IDENTITY, SAME_BASE_VARIANT, COMPATIBLE, ACCESSORY, SUBSTITUTE, DIFFERENT are different tasks. A different shoe size is positive for a base-product relation and negative for exact SKU identity.

SourceWhat it gives you

Negatives

Random “shoe vs refrigerator” negatives are too easy. The useful examples are model-year, capacity, pack-count, or visually near-identical confusers. Amazon's published 2023 system has a second hard-negative sharpening stage using target-category negatives.

“I'd use abundant weak pairs for broad representation learning, higher-confidence catalog relationships for the actual relation task, deliberately mined hard negatives, and an independent human-reviewed gold set because the existing catalog is itself noisy.”
Feedback loop

Active learning

Question: if human labels are expensive, which examples buy the most model improvement or risk reduction?
  • Uncertainty: near the decision boundary.
  • Disagreement: text vs image, cheap vs teacher, rule vs model, old vs new model.
  • Hard negatives: current nearest-neighbor mistakes.
  • Diversity: avoid labeling 10,000 versions of the same sneaker failure.
  • Novelty: new categories/languages/templates far from training data.
  • Business impact: uncertain high-volume items may deserve priority.
  • Random audit: catches confidently wrong blind spots that uncertainty sampling misses.

Measure quality gained per label, reduction in review volume at a fixed defect rate, and improvement on hard slices.

Core technical topic

What exactly are we training?

The embedding's first job is usually not final truth. It is: put the true candidate in the top K so a more expensive model can inspect it.

A real Amazon-published recipe (CIKM 2023)

  1. Train an image encoder and text encoder using InfoNCE contrastive learning on large catalog data.
  2. Image/text representations are projected into a shared space.
  3. Sharpen with harder negatives from a target category.
  4. Create one multimodal product vector by adding the image and text vectors and L2-normalizing.
  5. Use Amazon OpenSearch ANNS to fetch top-K nearest products.

InfoNCE intuition

For a batch of K products, build a K×K image/text similarity matrix. Correct pairs should be high; mismatched cross-pairs should be low. One batch gives many negatives without individually labeling each image/title relationship.

title1 title2 title3 a image1 HIGH low low image2 low HIGH low image3 low low HIGH

Identity-specific sharpening

Once broad representations exist, train on pairs that reflect the actual task: same exact product across sellers as positives, and near-identical but different model/capacity/pack-count products as hard negatives.

What would I put in one product vector? Start with title, brand, category, stable identity attributes, primary image, and perhaps a few representative supplemental images. Do not fill context with volatile price, promotions, or long low-information marketing copy just because the model permits it.

State of the art vocabulary

CLIP → SigLIP 2 → modern multimodal embedding/reranking

CLIP

Independent image and text towers trained contrastively into a shared vector space. Fast and precomputable; limited direct fine-grained interaction before the vector is produced.

SigLIP 2 (2025)

Modern multilingual vision-language encoder family with improved retrieval/semantic understanding, self-distillation, masked prediction, captioning pretraining, and better dense/local features.

Qwen3-VL-Embedding (2026)

Concrete open example of a unified multimodal embedding model: text/images/video or mixed input → one vector. 2B/8B releases, model spec up to 32K context, intended for first-stage retrieval.

Qwen3-VL-Reranker

Processes query/document together and returns a relevance score. It is the expensive precision-stage partner to the embedding model.

What does “unified” mean?

Visual inputs become visual tokens/representations and coexist with text in a multimodal transformer. Depending on architecture, modalities interact through explicit cross-attention or unified self-attention. The final contextual state can be pooled/extracted into one retrieval vector.

32K context does not mean use 32K

Context capacity is a ceiling, not a goal. Use ablations to determine marginal value of extra images/description text relative to compute and noise.

“I'd baseline a strong efficient encoder first—e.g. SigLIP-class visual representation plus text/attributes—then test a unified VLM embedding model on the hard slices. The newest model only wins if its quality gain justifies serving cost and complexity.”
Vocabulary to know cold

Bi-encoder / Siamese vs cross-encoder

Bi-encoder / dual tower

Product A → same/shared encoder → vector A Product B → same/shared encoder → vector B ↓ cosine / dot product

Bi means the two objects are encoded separately. This is closely related to Siamese/twin networks. The critical advantage: every catalog vector can be precomputed once.

Cross-encoder / reranker

[Product A text/images/attrs] + [Product B text/images/attrs] ↓ joint transformer ↓ relationship score

Much richer interactions, but you cannot precompute all pair scores. Therefore:

Bi-encoder asks: who is worth looking at?
Cross-encoder asks: now that I'm looking closely, what relationship actually holds?

ANN

Approximate nearest-neighbor indexes avoid exact similarity against every vector. Terms to recognize: HNSWIVFproduct quantizationOpenSearch k-NN. The tradeoff is retrieval recall vs latency/memory/cost.

Scientific rigor

How do we evaluate it?

Stage 1: retrieval

Recall@K is primary: is the true candidate somewhere in top K? If retrieval misses it, the reranker cannot recover it. Also measure latency, memory, and cost.

Stage 2: relationship decision

Precision, recall, PR curves, relation-specific confusion matrices. Amazon catalog research has used measures like recall at 95% precision, intuitive when bad relationships are costly.

Stage 3: calibration

If a score says 0.99, are about 99% of those decisions actually correct? Know reliability plots, ECE, Brier score, and temperature scaling/isotonic regression.

Stage 4: selective automation

automate 40% → extremely low error automate 70% → modest error automate 90% → larger error

Choose an operating point based on asymmetric business cost—not a magical 0.5 threshold.

Slices

Category, language/marketplace, seller cohort, identifier availability, image/text quality, head/tail, new products, relationship type. Average accuracy can hide catastrophic slices.

“I'd evaluate retrieval recall first, then relation precision/recall and calibration, then error-vs-coverage for the automation policy, and finally downstream catalog/customer outcomes.”

Modern GenAI

RAG and agents in this problem

Retrieval alone is not necessarily RAG. RAG retrieves evidence and supplies it to a generative model. Agents let a model conditionally choose tools/actions.

Where an agent might help

ambiguous candidate pair ↓ VLM notices model-number conflict/missing evidence ↓ query trusted catalog attributes ↓ still ambiguous → retrieve manufacturer/spec evidence ↓ compare → relation + evidence + confidence

Do not put an agent on every easy billion-scale decision simply because agents are fashionable. Use cheap deterministic/retrieval/model paths for volume and agentic evidence gathering for the ambiguous tail when extra information has value.

Evaluate: final relationship correctness, evidence/tool correctness, number of calls, cost/latency, loops, abstention, and unsafe actions.

Oral exam

Technical questions you should be able to answer

What is the point of the embedding?

High-recall candidate generation. Get the true relationship candidate into top K cheaply so an expensive model can inspect it.

Why “bi”-encoder?

Two objects are encoded independently. Often shared weights (Siamese/twin pattern). That permits precomputation and ANN indexing.

What does a cross-encoder buy?

Joint pair processing lets fine-grained features interact directly. Better precision, much more expensive, so use after retrieval.

Where do positives come from?

Naturally paired catalog data for broad contrastive representation learning, then trusted identity/relationship evidence and human-reviewed pairs for the task-specific layer.

What makes a good hard negative?

A visually/textually near-identical product that differs on the relationship-defining feature: generation, capacity, pack count, size, model number.

How does InfoNCE work?

In-batch similarity matrix: increase scores for matched positives and decrease scores for mismatches, yielding many negatives per batch.

How would you combine image and text?

Start with aligned independent vectors and weighted/additive fusion; compare learned projection/gating and unified VLM fusion on hard slices.

Why not dump the whole product page into a VLM?

More context can add noise and cost. Choose identity-bearing fields/images by ablation and marginal value, not context-window capacity.

What is Recall@K?

Fraction of true matches that appear among top K retrieved candidates. It sets a ceiling on the downstream reranker.

Precision or recall?

Both, but operating point depends on asymmetric cost. Exact-identity false merges may demand extremely high precision.

How do you calibrate?

Held-out gold data, reliability curves/ECE/Brier; temperature scaling or isotonic if needed; verify calibration by slice.

What is abstention/selective classification?

Automate only sufficiently trustworthy cases, route ambiguous ones to stronger inference/evidence/review.

How would you use active learning?

Uncertainty + model disagreement + hard-negative mining + diversity + novelty + impact, while retaining random audits for confident blind spots.

What is HNSW?

A graph-based approximate nearest-neighbor index that navigates hierarchical proximity links rather than scanning every vector.

What is product quantization?

Compress vector subspaces into codes, reducing memory/search cost at some retrieval-accuracy cost.

Batch vs online inference?

Batch maximizes throughput for large backfills; online/event processing handles new/changed products quickly. Mature catalog systems often need both.

Why use queues?

Absorb spikes, decouple stages, retry safely, and scale embedding/reranking/update workers independently.

Docker vs Kubernetes vs Terraform?

Docker packages; Kubernetes orchestrates running containers; Terraform/CDK/CloudFormation declares the infrastructure/resources they run on.

Where does distillation fit?

Use a strong expensive teacher to train a smaller embedding/reranking model that can operate at production scale.

Is retrieval + reranking RAG?

Not necessarily. RAG specifically uses retrieved evidence to condition a generative reasoning/output step.

Where would agents help?

The ambiguous tail where conditional tool/evidence gathering may change the decision; not necessarily the billion-scale easy path.

What if the existing catalog labels are noisy?

Use trusted tiers, weak supervision, human-reviewed gold data, label-source audits, robust evaluation, and don't conflate current catalog state with truth.

What would you do in your first month?

Clarify relationship semantics and error costs, establish/review the gold benchmark, reproduce a strong baseline, profile failure slices and production cost, then attack the biggest bottleneck.

What if an interviewer names a tool you haven't used?

“I haven't used that implementation directly. My understanding is that it solves X; I'd expect it to sit here in the architecture and I'd compare it against Y.” Never bluff hands-on experience.
